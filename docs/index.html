The fork. Turn It Up Gyros is the safest name nobody loves — highest mean, zero enthusiasm. Sumak is the only name anyone loves: one in three scored it 8+, consistently because sumac is a real spice, so it signals they know what they're doing. Its 47% rejection skews older and price-sensitive. Broad late-night positioning favors Turn It Up; a foodie-authenticity brand favors Sumak.

K Bob was rejected by 100% of the panel and repeatedly collided with K-Bob's Steakhouse. K Bab reads as a typo of kebab. Good Turn “sounds like a nonprofit or a bike shop” — it doesn't signal food.

The stated $13–16 combo is monthly-treat pricing; “steep for fast-casual” was the single most repeated objection across nearly every segment. The panel's implied move: a sub-$10 entry item to drive trial and frequency, with full combos as the upsell. Treat exact dollars as directional — the shape (weekly money is $8 money, not $14 money) was consistent across ages and incomes.

What substitutes for seeing it, ranked: “carved to order” language on the menu (286 of 300), the food itself proving it (283), reviews and word-of-mouth (197). Nobody asked for photos or a video screen. Strong signal: the closed kitchen doesn't threaten trust — put “carved to order” on the menu and let the food prove it. Worth a 20-person human sanity check before spending real money on structural changes either way.

Each participant is a persistent synthetic persona: demographics sampled from real Census block groups in the corridor, a dining psychographic mined from local discourse, and an occupation, household, and life details resolved for internal consistency and checked by an automated plausibility lint. The same 300 people answer every round, so results are comparable across tests.

Method & what to trust. 300 synthetic personas: demographics sampled from Census ACS block groups (Spokane County WA and Kootenai County ID, population-weighted, reproducible seed), dining psychographics and voice mined from roughly 3,400 real posts and comments on r/Spokane and r/CoeurdAlene, an LLM coherence pass resolving occupations, households, and life details into internally consistent people (checked by an automated plausibility lint), and surveys completed in-character with structured outputs. Robustness so far: the name ranking held across two independently built panels and two different models (13 of 15 pairwise orderings agree; every kill decision was unanimous). Research on synthetic panels shows strong aggregate agreement with human panels on directional linguistic tasks — names, claims, positioning — while individual-level correspondence is weaker and task-dependent; so this report gives you stable rankings and honest margins, not decimals, and the calls that involve real money get validated with real locals before spending.

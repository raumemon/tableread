Name test

The fork: Turn It Up Gyros is the safest name nobody loves (highest mean, 2% enthusiasm). Sumak is the only name that generates passion — 1 in 4 scored it 8+ for the same stated reason: sumac is a real spice, so it signals they know what they're doing. Its 45% rejection skews older and price-sensitive (“pretentious,” “how do you say it”). Broad late-night positioning → Turn It Up. Foodie-authenticity brand → Sumak.

K Bob was rejected by 100% of the panel and repeatedly collided with K-Bob's Steakhouse. K Bab reads as a typo of kebab. Good Turn “sounds like a nonprofit or a bike shop” — it doesn't signal food.

The stated $13–16 combo is monthly-treat pricing, and “steep for fast-casual” was the single most repeated objection across nearly every segment. The panel's implied move: a sub-$10 entry item (half gyro / mini combo) to drive trial and frequency, with full combos as the upsell. Treat exact dollar figures as directional; the shape — weekly money is $9 money, not $14 money — was consistent across ages and incomes.

What substitutes for seeing it, ranked by the panel: “carved to order” language on the menu (286 of 300), the food itself proving it (283), and reviews / word-of-mouth (197). Nobody asked for photos or a video screen of the spit. The closed kitchen costs nothing if the menu makes the claim and the product backs it up.

Method & honest caveats. 300 synthetic personas: demographics sampled from Census ACS block groups (Spokane County WA + Kootenai County ID, population-weighted, reproducible seed), dining psychographics and voice mined from ~3,400 real posts and comments on r/Spokane and r/CoeurdAlene, surveys completed in-character by claude-sonnet-5 with structured outputs. Published validations put well-grounded synthetic panels at 85–95% agreement with human panels on directional, linguistic questions — names, claims, positioning — which is what this report contains. Synthetic panels are not a substitute for human research on taste, final pricing, or major-spend go/no-go decisions; errors are correlated across personas, so treat margins, not means, with confidence. Total research cost of this round: about $7.
